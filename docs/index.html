Built around how Tennessee eats

Star ratings won't tell you which shack fries the hot chicken everyone lines up for, or which barbecue joint still cooks the whole hog. Tennessee Eats is organized the way people here eat: hot chicken, Memphis barbecue, whole hog, meat-and-threes, biscuits and country ham, and Smokies pancake houses, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Every place links to the state's own inspection reports, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out in the mountains.

What stays on your phone

Tennessee Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Tennessee Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The hot chicken, Memphis barbecue, whole hog, meat-and-three, biscuit, pancake house and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: its website or menu, a dated local news story or an official tourism listing. The wider directory of 14,862 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against Nashville's beer permits, and adds restaurants from that list.

Where can I get Nashville hot chicken?

Our hot chicken list has 16 hand-checked places in 3 towns, most of them in Nashville, from Prince's to Bolton's and Hattie B's. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Tennessee restaurant's inspection?

The Tennessee Department of Health and the health departments of Davidson, Shelby, Knox, Hamilton and Madison counties post each inspection report, with its score, in one search. It isn't published as data, so we link it from every place rather than copy it, and we compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

Only in Nashville. Metro Nashville's Beer Permit Board publishes its permits, so a Davidson County place with an on-site beer permit says so. Elsewhere, beer is permitted town by town and liquor licenses aren't published as a list, so alcohol shows as unknown, never as no.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
